     Cosmic radiation comes from our sun and other stars in the form
of particles, such as protons, and electromagnetic waves, such as
X-rays.  These energetic rays stream through our bodies all the time,
though Earth's atmosphere deflects most of them before they reach
us.  The higher you go in altitude, from say New York to Denver, the
less atmosphere there is to protect you, and the more radiation hits
you.  In an airplane, according to Barish's new paper, exposure levels
are several hundred times higher than they are on the ground.
