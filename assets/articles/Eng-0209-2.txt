     PCBs, which were once commonly used in pesticides and a wide
variety of industrial fluids, have been banned for decades.  But with
very slow breakdown times, they are still widespread in the environment,
particularly in more developed areas.  In fish, birds, minks and other
creatures, PCBs have been linked with slower growth rates, particularly
in young animals and developing embryos.
