     Physicists have succeeded for the first time in trapping atoms
of anti-matter hydrogen, or antihydrogen.  The feat takes researchers
one step closer to seeing how antihydrogen might differ from normal
hydrogen.  That, in turn, could reveal all sorts of things about gravity
and perhaps shed light on what happened to all the antimatter that
theoretically should be, but isn't, present in the universe.
     What's behind this leap second?  The ever-so-slight slowing of
Earth's rotation, or the 24-hour spin that brings the sun into our skies
every morning.  Earth has been falling behind the atomic time at a rate
of about 2 million-seconds per day, currently trailing the atomic time
by six-tenths of a second.  Every now and then a leap second must be
added to atomic clocks to keep in sync with Earth's oddball rotation.
