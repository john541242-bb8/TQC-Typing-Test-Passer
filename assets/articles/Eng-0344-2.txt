     Planning means deciding what you will do and when you will do
it.  Calendars, lists, and schedules can help you plan.  You can buy or
draw a calendar and keep it near your workspace.  Making a schedule or
a "to-do" list for yourself is a good idea.  Looking at your list helps
you keep track of what you need to do.  Add new things as you get
assignments, and check off things when you've done them.  Use your
list to decide which thing is most important to work on first.
     Most experts today do not demand 2 hours of daily exercise.  The
U.S. Department of Health and Human Services recommends that people get
at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous physical activity most days
of the week.  Brisk walking, jogging, and swimming are easy ways to
boost your heart rate and promote cardiovascular health.  Exercise can
also help lower blood pressure and counter stress.
     Once you recognize that you're feeling stressed, there are several
things you can do.  You can try talking about what's bothering you with
an adult you trust, like a parent or teacher.  Bring up what's been on
your mind and how it makes you feel.  An adult may have ideas about how
to solve whatever is worrying or making you uncomfortable.
