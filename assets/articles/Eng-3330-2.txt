     Major, explosive volcanic eruptions send tons of gaseous sulfur
compounds high into the atmosphere.  These compounds chemically
form minute sulfate aerosol particles that together act like a parasol,
defecting incoming solar radiation and temporarily cooling the planet.
This natural effect has inspired the "geoengineering" idea that humans
could arrest the progress of global warming by pumping a bunch of
sulfur dioxide into the atmosphere.  Researchers, however, warn that
the impact of such a scheme may not be so simple, or predicable.
     Sergey Bulat of Petersburg Nuclear Physics Institute in Russia
presented preliminary results from a study of ice pulled from the
6,000-square-mile subglacial lake in February.  He and his colleagues
told the 12th European Workshop on Astrobiology that they found fewer
than 10 microbes per milliliter, according to a report at Nature.
