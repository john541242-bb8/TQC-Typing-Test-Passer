     Scientists suspected they were hearing vibrations in the dunes'
subsurface layers.  In 2009, however, University of France researchers
found that the sound is created by vibrations of the sand grains that
avalanche down the dunes.  In a new study to be published Friday in
the journal Geophysical Research Letters, those French scientists
tackle another mystery:  How can singing sands create multiple notes
at the same time?  To answer that question, scientists studied sound
produced at two different dunes:  one in the Saharain southwestern
Morocco, and one near Al-Askharah, a coastal town in southeastern
Oman, on the Arabian Peninsula.
