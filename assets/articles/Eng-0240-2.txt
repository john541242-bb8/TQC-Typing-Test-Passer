     Bates, a researcher in the University of Liverpool's Department
of Musculoskeletal Biology, and colleague Peter Falkingham of the
University of Manchester artificially scaled up the skulls of a human,
an alligator, a juvenile T. rex, and Allosaurus to the size of an adult
T. rex.  In each case, the bite forces increases as expected, but they
did not increase to the level of the adult T. rex, suggesting that this
formidable predator had the most powerful bite of any land animal.
