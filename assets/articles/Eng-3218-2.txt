     Like many parasites, the human liver fluke has a complicated life
span.  An egg is first released from the human host.  A snail eats the
egg, which hatches and begins to develop.  Eventually a free swimming
larvae emerges from the snail and attaches to the skin of a freshwater
fish.  When a human eats the fish raw, or as most people in Thailand are
infected, by eating a delicacy of fermented fish, the parasite emerges
from the small intestine and takes up residence inside the liver.
