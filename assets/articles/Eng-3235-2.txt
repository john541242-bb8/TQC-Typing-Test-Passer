     Monuments Fund issues its watch list every two years.  The list
was initiated in the mid-1990s to call attention to sites that are
threatened by neglect, vandalism, conflict or disaster.  It's assembled
by a panel of experts in archaeology, architecture, art history and
preservation.  The last list, in 2007, was the first to add global
warming to the roster of forces the monuments fund says are threatening
humanity's architectural and cultural heritage.
