     When water gets lofted into Mars' upper atmosphere, solar radiation
can break it apart into hydrogen (or deuterium) and oxygen.  Hydrogen
escapes faster because it is lighter than deuterium.  Since the lighter
version escapes more often, over time, the Martian atmosphere has less
and less hydrogen compared to the amount of deuterium remaining.  The
Martian atmosphere therefore becomes richer and richer in deuterium.
