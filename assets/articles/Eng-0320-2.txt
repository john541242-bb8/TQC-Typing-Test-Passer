     In legend, the bright sun was a dazzling temptation for Icarus
and so, too, it is for NASA astronaut Sunita Williams, who appears
to touch our closest star in a photo snapped during a spacewalk this
week.  But unlike Icarus, the boy who flew too close to the sun and
fell back to Earth when his wax wings melted, Williams revels in the
sun's bright light while floating outside the International Space
Station.  The image was taken during a spacewalk to finish repairs
on a vital power unit on Wednesday (Sept. 5).

     While Earth spins on its axis, which pops out at the north and
south geographical poles, it's hurtling through space in an annual
orbit around the sun.  If we measure the angle of the rotation with
respect to the plane of the orbit around the sun, we see it's tilted
23.5 degrees from vertical.  The tilt of the axis is responsible for
the seasons we experience as the northern and southern hemispheres
are alternately presented toward the sun throughout the year.
