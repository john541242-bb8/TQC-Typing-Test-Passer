     2008 may be the year with the lowest Arctic sea ice volume on
record, say researchers.  Ice volume (for which depth is most important)
is harder to calculate than ice extent (a measure of acreage), but it
speaks volumes about how well the ice will fare year-to-year.  The
weakening ice this year was easy to see from space.  This July 28,
2008, image shows the shattering Canadian ice to the east, looking
into the Northwest Passage.  The image was created with data from
NASA's Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer (MODIS) sensor.
